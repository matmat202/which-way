Chapter 2 · Is that just a few big jumps?

Shuffle the months

Keep every month-to-month move the rate ever made, the huge ones included, and shuffle their order. Do it times. Each grey dot is one shuffle: how often its years ended higher, and how big its rises were next to its falls.

The typical shuffle rises in of years, with rises times its falls. Not one of the rises as rarely and as hard as the real rate. The nearest any gets is of years, and times. So the shape comes from the order of the months, not from the size of the jumps.

Chapter 3 · The rest of the game

Only one other number does it

Which Way Next? asks about numbers. Here are the other , shuffled 200 times each the same way. Each tail starts where that number's own typical shuffle sits.

Interest rates, oil, house building and the rest go every which way. For of them, at least one shuffle in twenty is as lopsided as the real line. Only the is like unemployment: none of its shuffles is as lopsided as it is.

Coda · What to guess

Guess lower, expect to be wrong loudly

The usual explanation is that firms can lay people off in a month, but hiring them back takes years. Economists have measured the shape for a long time: Salih Neftçi asked it of this very series in 1984, in Are economic time series asymmetric over the business cycle?

So in Which Way Next?, "lower" is the better bet on unemployment. When it loses, it loses big.
